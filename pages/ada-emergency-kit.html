Survival Briefing

Starting a new job can be stressful, especially if you have a disability or medical condition that may affect your work.

Understanding your workplace rights and available accommodations can help you focus on performing your job successfully.

Emergency Kit Contents

📜 Know Your Rights

The ADA helps protect qualified individuals with disabilities from discrimination in the workplace.

  • Equal employment opportunities
  • Protection from discrimination
  • Access to reasonable accommodations

🧰 Workplace Accommodations

Accommodations are workplace adjustments that help employees perform essential job functions.

  • Assistive technology
  • Modified workspaces
  • Schedule adjustments
  • Additional support resources

🗂 Documentation

Keeping records of accommodation requests and workplace communications can be helpful.

Disclosure Decision Tree

Deciding whether to disclose a disability is a personal decision.

Consider:

  • Do you need workplace accommodations?
  • Would disclosure improve your ability to perform your job?
  • Do you understand your options and available resources?

Common Workplace Myths

Myth

"Requesting accommodations means I'm not capable."

Reality

Accommodations exist to help qualified employees perform their jobs successfully.

Myth

"Everyone will automatically understand my needs."

Reality

Communication and self-advocacy are often important parts of the accommodation process.
