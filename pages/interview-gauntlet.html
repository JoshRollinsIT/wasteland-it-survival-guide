Pre-Interview Checklist

Important Character Stats

Many recruits assume technical knowledge is everything. In reality, employers often evaluate:

  • Communication - Can you explain technical issues clearly?
  • Professionalism - Are you reliable and respectful?
  • Teamwork - Can you work effectively with others?
  • Problem Solving - How do you approach challenges?
  • Adaptability - Can you learn new technologies?

Boss Battle #1: Tell Me About Yourself

Common Mistake

Giving your entire life story.

Better Strategy

Focus on:

  • Relevant experience
  • Technical interests
  • Career goals

Example Response

I've worked in roles involving security, administration, customer service, and problem solving. Recently I have focused on developing my technical skills through CompTIA A+ training and technology-focused coursework. I'm looking for an IT role where I can continue learning while helping users solve problems.

HR Overseer Tip

"Keep your answer between one and two minutes. If your answer requires a lunch break, it's too long."

Boss Battle #2: What Is Your Greatest Strength?

Choose a real strength and support it with evidence.

Examples:

  • Troubleshooting
  • Documentation
  • Customer Service
  • Reliability
  • Learning New Technology

Example Response

One of my greatest strengths is documentation. In previous positions I regularly created procedures, maintained records, and ensured information was organized so issues could be resolved efficiently.

Boss Battle #3: What Is Your Greatest Weakness?

Warning

Avoid saying:

  • "I don't have any weaknesses."
  • "I'm a perfectionist."

Better Strategy

Choose a genuine challenge and explain how you are improving.

Example Response

Earlier in my career I sometimes waited too long before asking for help. Over time I've learned that collaborating sooner often leads to faster solutions and better outcomes.

The STAR Protocol

Use the STAR Method for behavioral interview questions.

  • S - Situation
  • T - Task
  • A - Action
  • R - Result

Example

Situation: A process was creating delays for users.

Task: Determine the cause and improve efficiency.

Action: Gathered information, documented findings, and proposed changes.

Result: The process improved and future issues were reduced.

Random Encounter: You Don't Know the Answer

An interviewer asks a technical question you've never heard before.

Wrong Response

"Uhhh... I know it, I just can't explain it."

Better Response

"I'm not certain of the answer, but here's how I would research and troubleshoot the issue."

In IT, demonstrating critical thinking is often more valuable than memorizing every answer.

HR Overseer Alert

"Nobody knows everything. Show your thought process and willingness to learn."
